Information for remote workers coming to Koh Lanta, Thailand

Visa and immigration

Last updated November 19, 2026

Most visitors get 30 days visa-free in Thailand, and you can extend that once by another 30 days. There’s no immigration office on Koh Lanta itself, so extensions mean a trip to Krabi Town. Visa agents on the island can help with the paperwork.

⚠️ Changed on 15 September 2026: Thailand cut visa-free stays from 60 to 30 days and shortened the list of eligible countries to 60 countries and territories (official notice in the Royal Gazette, in Thai; English list). A lot of websites still say 60 days. Check that your country is on the list before you book.

Want to stay longer from the start? Apply for a tourist visa before your trip. It gives you 60 days per entry.

Before you fly, fill out the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC). It’s required for every arrival, within 3 days before you land.

Is there an immigration office on Koh Lanta? #

No. Koh Lanta is part of Krabi province, so extensions and other immigration paperwork go through the Krabi Immigration Office in Krabi Town on the mainland. Thai Immigration requires you to apply for an extension in person, but one of the visa agents on the island can prepare the paperwork and arrange the trip.

Extending your stay #

A 30-day extension costs 1,900 THB, paid in cash, and you apply in person at the immigration office. A visa-free stay can only be extended once. Bring:

  • Your passport, plus copies of the photo page and your entry stamp
  • A passport photo
  • The address where you’re staying (your accommodation registers you with immigration)
  • The TM.7 extension form, which you can fill out at the office

Visa agents on Koh Lanta #

They can handle extensions, 90-day reports, and re-entry permits. You give them your passport and they bring it back done. For an extension you still have to show up at the immigration office yourself.

Visa runs #

The closest border crossing is Wang Prachan in Satun, on the Malaysian border. Border-run vans from Krabi do the trip there and back in a day. Leaving and coming back in gets you a fresh 30-day stamp, but visa-exempt visitors can only enter Thailand over land twice per calendar year. Ask your visa agent or accommodation for current options.

Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) #

If you want to stay and work remotely for months, look into the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV). It’s a 5-year multiple-entry visa with stays of up to 180 days, which you can extend once by another 180 days. The fee is 10,000 THB and you need 500,000 THB in savings.

Since 31 August 2026 you have to apply at the Thai embassy for the country you’re a citizen or legal resident of, and you need a criminal record certificate.
